\section{Proofs and Derivations}
\label{app:proofs}

\subsection{Proof of Proposition \ref{prop:nash-set}}

From \eqref{eq:local-payoff}, for any $x_j\in[0,1]$,
\[
W_i(x_i,x_j)
=W_i(0,x_j)
+x_i\left(\Delta+\alpha A-Ax_j\right).
\]
Thus $W_i$ is affine in $x_i$ and the coefficient on $x_i$ determines the global best response. Setting that coefficient equal to zero gives $x_j=q=\alpha+\Delta/A$, which yields \eqref{eq:best-response}.

If $A<A^N=\Delta/(1-\alpha)$, then $q>1$. Hence $x_j<q$ for every feasible $x_j$, so each player's unique best response is $1$ and $(1,1)$ is the unique Nash equilibrium.

If $A=A^N$, then $q=1$. A player strictly prefers $1$ whenever the rival chooses less than $1$ and is indifferent over all $x_i\in[0,1]$ when the rival chooses $1$. Therefore a profile is a Nash equilibrium if and only if at least one component equals $1$.

If $A>A^N$, then $q\in(0,1)$. Suppose first that $x_2<q$. Then player 1's unique best response is $x_1=1$. Since $1>q$, player 2's unique best response to $x_1=1$ is $x_2=0$, giving $(1,0)$. The symmetric argument gives $(0,1)$ when $x_2>q$. Finally, if one player chooses exactly $q$, the other may choose any action, but the first player can itself be willing to choose $q$ only if the other also chooses $q$. Hence $(q,q)$ is the only remaining equilibrium. This proves the exact set.

\subsection{Proof of Proposition \ref{prop:planner}}

Ignoring the constant $2b_D$, aggregate welfare is
\[
\widetilde W(x_1,x_2)
=(\Delta+A)(x_1+x_2)-2Ax_1x_2.
\]
This objective is bilinear, so a global maximum on the compact rectangle $[0,1]^2$ is attained at a corner unless the objective is flat along an edge. The corner values, net of $2b_D$, are
\[
\widetilde W(0,0)=0,\qquad
\widetilde W(1,0)=\widetilde W(0,1)=\Delta+A,
\qquad
\widetilde W(1,1)=2\Delta.
\]
If $A<\Delta$, then $2\Delta>\Delta+A>0$, so $(1,1)$ is the unique optimum. If $A>\Delta$, then $\Delta+A>2\Delta$, so $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$ are the exact optima. At $A=\Delta$,
\[
\widetilde W(x_1,x_2)
=2A\left[x_1+x_2-x_1x_2\right]
=2A\left[1-(1-x_1)(1-x_2)\right],
\]
which reaches its maximum if and only if $x_1=1$ or $x_2=1$.

\subsection{Proof of Proposition \ref{prop:threshold-ordering}}

Since $0<\alpha<1$,
\[
\frac{1}{1-\alpha}>1.
\]
Multiplying by $\Delta>0$ gives
\[
A^N=\frac{\Delta}{1-\alpha}>\Delta=A^P.
\]
Subtracting $A^P$ yields
\[
A^N-A^P
=\frac{\Delta}{1-\alpha}-\Delta
=\frac{\alpha\Delta}{1-\alpha}>0.
\]

\subsection{Proof of Proposition \ref{prop:duplication-wedge}}

If $\Delta<A<\Delta/(1-\alpha)$, then $A>A^P$ and $A<A^N$. Proposition \ref{prop:planner} therefore implies that the coordinated optima are $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$, while Proposition \ref{prop:nash-set} implies that $(1,1)$ is the unique Nash equilibrium. The welfare difference is
\[
W(1,0)-W(1,1)
=(2b_D+\Delta+A)-(2b_D+2\Delta)
=A-\Delta>0.
\]

\subsection{The symmetric high-$A$ equilibrium}

For $A>A^N$, $q=\alpha+\Delta/A\in(0,1)$. Direct substitution into \eqref{eq:aggregate-welfare} and simplification using $\Delta=A(q-\alpha)$ gives
\[
W(1,0)-W(q,q)
=A\left[(1-\alpha)(1-q)+\alpha q\right].
\]
Every factor inside the brackets is positive for $0<\alpha,q<1$, proving \eqref{eq:highA-gap}.

\subsection{Proof of Proposition \ref{prop:switching-lemma}}

Relative to duplication, coordinated reallocation sacrifices direct surplus $\Delta$ and creates total complementary surplus $G$. Its net gain is $G-\Delta$, giving condition \eqref{eq:planner-G}. The reallocating jurisdiction captures only $\lambda G$, so its private net gain is $\lambda G-\Delta$, giving \eqref{eq:local-G}. The two thresholds in \eqref{eq:G-thresholds} follow immediately. Because $0<\lambda<1$, $\Delta/\lambda>\Delta$.

\subsection{Restricted constant-returns production result}

Let $F$ satisfy the conditions in Section \ref{sec:robustness}. Homogeneity of degree one and differentiability imply Euler's identity
\[
F(1,1)=F_u(1,1)+F_d(1,1).
\]
The coordinated gain from creating a unit complementary match is $AF(1,1)$, while the $D$ jurisdiction's competitive factor income is $AF_d(1,1)$. Hence its local switching threshold is $\Delta/F_d(1,1)$ and the coordinated threshold is $\Delta/F(1,1)$. Since $F_u(1,1)>0$, $F(1,1)>F_d(1,1)$, which implies $A_F^P<A_F^N$.

\subsection{Alternative matching identity and local best response}

Let $s=x_1+x_2$. If $s\leq1$, then $x_1\leq1-x_2$ and $x_2\leq1-x_1$, so
\[
M(x_1,x_2)=x_1+x_2=s.
\]
If $s\geq1$, then $1-x_2\leq x_1$ and $1-x_1\leq x_2$, so
\[
M(x_1,x_2)=2-s.
\]
Therefore
\[
M(x_1,x_2)=1-|s-1|=1-|x_1+x_2-1|.
\]

Fix rival allocation $x_j=y$. Region $i$'s nonconstant payoff under the incidence rule in \eqref{eq:capacity-local-income} is
\[
\Delta x_i+(1-\lambda)A\min\{x_i,1-y\}+\lambda A\min\{1-x_i,y\}.
\]
For $x_i\leq1-y$, its slope is
\[
\Delta+(1-\lambda)A>0.
\]
For $x_i\geq1-y$, its slope is
\[
\Delta-\lambda A.
\]
Hence if $A<\Delta/\lambda$, the payoff is strictly increasing over the full feasible interval and $x_i=1$ is the unique best response to every $y$. Thus $(1,1)$ is the unique decentralized equilibrium in that region.

For a planner objective proportional to $\Delta s+A M$, the objective is increasing in $s$ on $s\leq1$ and has slope $\Delta-A$ on $s\geq1$. Thus, when $A>\Delta$, the coordinated optimum set is $s=1$. Combining the two conditions yields the robustness wedge $\Delta<A<\Delta/\lambda$ used in Section \ref{sec:robustness}.
