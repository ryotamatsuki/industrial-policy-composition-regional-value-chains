\begin{abstract}
This paper studies how regional governments allocate a fixed industrial-policy capacity across activities that are complementary across jurisdictions. Each government privately favors a locally high-return activity, while assigning capacity to the complementary activity creates cross-regional value that the assigning region captures only partially. In the baseline continuous portfolio game, the coordinated fixed-capacity planner reallocates away from duplication when cross-regional complementarity exceeds $A^P=\Delta$, whereas differentiated decentralized equilibria first appear only at $A^N=\Delta/(1-\alpha)$. Hence $A^P<A^N$, and for a positive-measure parameter region complete priority duplication is the unique Nash equilibrium even though coordinated reallocation of the same total policy capacity is strictly welfare improving. A binary switching representation isolates incomplete local capture as the source of the threshold wedge. Restricted constant-returns and alternative-matching exercises preserve the threshold logic while clarifying that complete regional specialization is baseline-specific. The contribution is a composition-specific equilibrium characterization for fixed industrial-policy portfolios, not a general novelty claim about interjurisdictional spillovers.
\end{abstract}
