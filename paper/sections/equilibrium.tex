\section{Decentralized Equilibrium}
\label{sec:equilibrium}

Because \eqref{eq:local-payoff} is affine in $x_i$, the global comparison between $x_i=1$ and $x_i=0$ determines the entire best-response correspondence. The payoff difference is
\begin{equation}
W_i(1,x_j)-W_i(0,x_j)
=\Delta+\alpha A-Ax_j.
\label{eq:payoff-difference}
\end{equation}
Define
\begin{equation}
q\equiv \alpha+\frac{\Delta}{A}
\label{eq:q}
\end{equation}
and the decentralized threshold
\begin{equation}
A^N\equiv \frac{\Delta}{1-\alpha}.
\label{eq:AN}
\end{equation}
The global best-response correspondence is
\begin{equation}
BR_i(x_j)=
\begin{cases}
1, & x_j<q,\\
[0,1], & x_j=q,\\
0, & x_j>q.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:best-response}
\end{equation}
The marginal attractiveness of $U$ falls one-for-one with the rival's allocation to $U$:
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_j}
\left[W_i(1,x_j)-W_i(0,x_j)\right]=-A<0.
\label{eq:strategic-substitutes}
\end{equation}
Thus policy portfolios are strategic substitutes in the baseline game. Cross-regional complementarity creates an incentive to move toward $D$ when the other region concentrates on $U$, but this force must overcome the direct local premium $\Delta$.

\begin{proposition}[Exact decentralized equilibrium set]
\label{prop:nash-set}
For $\Delta>0$, $A>0$, and $0<\alpha<1$, the Nash equilibria are characterized as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $0<A<A^N$, the unique equilibrium is $(x_1,x_2)=(1,1)$.
\item If $A=A^N$, the equilibrium set is
\[
\{(x_1,x_2)\in[0,1]^2:x_1=1\ \text{or}\ x_2=1\}.
\]
\item If $A>A^N$, the exact equilibrium set is
\[
\{(1,0),(0,1),(q,q)\},
\]
where $q$ is given by \eqref{eq:q} and satisfies $q\in(0,1)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

The low-$A$ region is especially important. Even though $D$ is technologically complementary to the rival region's $U$ activity, each government strictly prefers to allocate all policy capacity to its own high-return activity. Only once $A$ exceeds $A^N$ do differentiated equilibria appear. At the same time, a symmetric interior equilibrium also emerges; high complementarity therefore does not eliminate equilibrium multiplicity or guarantee that every decentralized equilibrium is efficient.
