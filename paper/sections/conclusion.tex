\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Industrial-policy coordination can matter even when governments do not change the total amount of support. This paper isolates a compositional channel. When regional governments allocate scarce policy capacity across complementary activities, the jurisdiction that gives up a locally attractive priority may capture only part of the cross-regional value created by doing so. Decentralized and coordinated incentives then switch at different thresholds.

In the baseline continuous portfolio game, the coordinated fixed-capacity threshold is $A^P=\Delta$ and the decentralized threshold is $A^N=\Delta/(1-\alpha)$. The strict ordering $A^P<A^N$ creates a positive-measure region in which complete priority duplication is the unique Nash equilibrium while coordinated reallocation is strictly welfare improving. The distortion is therefore neither an over-spending result nor, in that region, an equilibrium-selection problem.

The robustness analysis identifies both the reach and the boundary of the mechanism. A constant-capture switching representation preserves the threshold wedge whenever the reallocating jurisdiction captures less than the total complementary value it creates. Concave constant-returns production and a second matching technology support the same incidence logic. At the same time, endogenous capture can eliminate a finite decentralized switching threshold, and alternative matching shows that coordinated reallocation need not imply complete regional specialization. These limitations are part of the result rather than qualifications to be hidden.

The model therefore supports a narrow policy message: where regional priority choices create material complementary value outside the jurisdiction, coordination of policy composition can improve welfare without increasing total policy capacity. Whether such coordination should be centralized, decentralized through transfers, or implemented through interregional institutions is a separate design question that requires additional primitives and is not resolved here.
