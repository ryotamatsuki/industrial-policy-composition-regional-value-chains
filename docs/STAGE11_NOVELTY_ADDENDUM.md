# Stage 11 Novelty Addendum — Fixed-Budget Composition Prior Art

Canonical freeze: `IPCRVC-THEORY-FREEZE-2026-09-07-v1`.

During the final hostile novelty search, the audit identified an additional close component paper:

- Katrina Kosec and Tewodaj Mogues (2020), “Public Investment Choices by Local and Central Governments,” *The World Bank Economic Review* 34(Supplement 1): S52–S57, DOI `10.1093/wber/lhz010`.

## Why it matters

Kosec and Mogues explicitly model a fixed government budget normalized to one and a portfolio share allocated between two sectors, with the residual allocated to the other sector. This means that neither a fixed budget nor a two-sector composition choice can be claimed as novel components of the present paper.

## Why it does not absorb the current whole game

The Kosec–Mogues model compares provision under a local government with provision under a central government in an environment driven by information and government-priority differences. It does not model two regional governments simultaneously choosing interacting portfolios, and it does not generate the present paper's cross-regional U–D complementarity game or the exact Nash/planner Priority Duplication Wedge.

Accordingly, the Stage-11 classification is:

`COMPONENT OVERLAP / IMPORTANT CLOSE BENCHMARK — NOT EXACT PRIOR ART`

This result strengthens the required novelty calibration. The surviving claim must not be “fixed-budget policy composition.” It is the strategic combination of:

1. multiple regional governments;
2. fixed local policy portfolios;
3. different vertically complementary activities across jurisdictions;
4. incomplete jurisdictional capture of complementary surplus; and
5. an exact positive-measure interval with unique decentralized duplication versus coordinated reallocation.

The Related Literature section now cites and distinguishes Kosec and Mogues (2020). No theory change follows from this addendum.
