## 1. Current journal evidence

### 1.1 Journal of Regional Science — PRIMARY

Current official scope says JRS publishes original analytical research at the intersection of economics and quantitative geography, explicitly including rigorous methodological contributions and seminal theoretical pieces, and research on policies related to cities and regions.

Current journal page (2026) reports an 8% acceptance rate and a 14-day median submission-to-first-decision statistic. These are publisher-reported journal metrics, not estimates produced for this project; they are not used as the main reason for journal choice.

The current author guide states:

- no submission fee;
- Free Format Submission for initial manuscripts;
- double-blind peer review;
- ORCID required;
- abstract up to 250 words;
- seven keywords;
- a separate title page with author details;
- standard ethics/integrity statements as applicable.

Recent publication evidence confirms that JRS still publishes analytical and theoretical regional-economics work, including:

- *The Core-Periphery Model Under Additively Separable Preferences* (2025), a formal theoretical contribution to economic geography/agglomeration;
- *How Should Place-Based Policies Be Designed to Efficiently Promote Retail Agglomeration?* (2025), combining explicit theoretical and welfare analysis of place-based policy;
- *Autonomous Vehicles, Urban Deployment, and Welfare Effects* (2026), an analytical urban-spatial model;
- current place-based-policy and local-government articles in 2025–2026.

**Fit assessment:** strongest. The present paper is an analytical regional-policy paper with an exact equilibrium/planner comparison and a regional-government mechanism. It does not need to acquire data or a quantitative spatial equilibrium to become recognizable as a JRS paper.

**Primary desk risk:** novelty ceiling. A JRS editor may view the model as too small once classic fiscal-spillover and fixed-budget composition prior art are acknowledged. The defense must be the exact configuration theorem, not the generic externality story.

### 1.2 Journal of Urban Economics — STRETCH, NOT FIRST SUBMISSION

Current official scope states that JUE welcomes theoretical or empirical, positive or normative work of great scholarly merit in urban economics.

Recent JUE papers show a high current bar for local-policy questions. Examples include:

- *Local causes and aggregate implications of land use regulation* (2023), with an endogenous local-regulation mechanism embedded in a quantitative spatial equilibrium model;
- *Local favoritism in China's public procurement* (2025), with detailed microdata and institutional identification;
- *Strategies and consequences of local fiscal consolidation* (2025), causal empirical analysis of municipal fiscal policy;
- *The local and aggregate effects of land-use regulation on farmland protection* (online 2026), combining causal evidence and a quantitative spatial model.

JUE still accepts pure theory in principle, but the current manuscript has no endogenous location, commuting, land market, migration, transport-cost, or spatial-equilibrium margin. Its “space” is jurisdictional rather than urban-geographic.

**Fit assessment:** conceptually plausible but contribution threshold and urban/spatial ownership are both materially weaker than JRS.

**Stage-12 decision:** do not submit to JUE first. Doing so would amount to a prestige lottery rather than evidence-based positioning. A JUE attempt would only be rational if the authors deliberately accept a high desk-reject risk without changing the model.

### 1.3 Journal of Public Economics — REJECT AS CURRENT TARGET

Current official scope welcomes original public-economics contributions with emphasis on modern economic theory and quantitative analysis and policy relevance to an international readership.

The topic is within scope, but the audience makes the Stage-11 novelty problem worse. Classic interjurisdictional spillover/local-public-good results are core public-economics prior art, and current JPubE local-government work is often substantially richer. For example, *Tournament-style political competition and local protectionism: Theory and evidence from China* (2025) combines a model of interjurisdictional resource-allocation distortion with empirical evidence; recent issues also contain modern fiscal-federalism and local strategic-policy papers.

**Fit assessment:** scope fit but contribution-threshold mismatch.

**Kill-test result:** current paper should not target JPubE. To make JPubE a credible primary target would likely require a new public-finance mechanism, endogenous transfers/taxes, decentralization design, or materially broader theorem. Stage 12 is prohibited from adding such theory.

### 1.4 Regional Science and Urban Economics — REALISTIC FALLBACK

Current official scope states that RSUE publishes significant theoretical or empirical, positive or normative contributions on important regional and urban economics issues and solicits original papers with a spatial dimension of interest to economists.

A directly relevant recent theoretical example is *Spatial externalities, R&D spillovers, and endogenous technological change* (2024), which compares equilibrium and optimal spatial allocations in a theoretical growth setting with spatial spillovers. RSUE is also the only journal surfaced by Elsevier under its “Regional government analysis” category.

**Fit assessment:** strong. The current paper's local-government game, cross-jurisdiction interaction, equilibrium/planner comparison and policy interpretation fit the journal well.

**Why not primary over JRS:** RSUE's strongest theory papers often possess a more explicit spatial mechanism—distance, continuous geography, location, mobility, transport or spatial equilibrium. The current paper's regional dimension is strategic-jurisdictional rather than spatial in that stronger sense. JRS is more naturally tolerant of an analytical regional-policy theorem whose spatial structure is deliberately minimal.

**Fallback role:** submit to RSUE if JRS rejects for contribution magnitude, generality, or journal ceiling rather than correctness.

### 1.5 Journal of Economic Geography — ALTERNATIVE TRACK

Current official scope welcomes work across empirical and theoretical perspectives, including conceptual/theory-development papers. The journal currently emphasizes industrial policy, regions, global production/value chains, innovation and regional development. Its 2026 content includes work on subnational policy and industrial upgrading in global value chains, and its recent calls have explicitly welcomed theoretical and empirical work on the state reshaping economic geographies.

Current author guidelines specify for standard articles:

- maximum 8,000 words excluding references;
- abstract no more than 100 words;
- four keywords and JEL codes;
- Economics versus Geography section selection;
- Economics-section submission fee of USD 75;
- double-anonymous review;
- mathematical exposition must remain intelligible to a cross-disciplinary readership.

**Fit assessment:** topical fit is very strong, especially value chains, smart specialization and subnational industrial policy. However, the current manuscript is written primarily as formal economics and does not yet engage deeply with the economic-geography conceptual literature. A JoEG submission would require substantial presentation/literature integration, although no theory change.

**Role:** alternative track rather than automatic fallback. Use it if a strategic decision is made to foreground economic-geography debates and keep the standard article within the 8,000-word limit.

## 3. Primary target rationale — Journal of Regional Science

JRS is selected because it minimizes the amount of journal-induced distortion required to the paper.

The paper already has the features JRS explicitly says it values:

1. analytical regional economics;
2. a clear regional-policy question;
3. exact equilibrium and welfare comparison;
4. a spatial/jurisdictional interaction across regions;
5. place-based/industrial-policy relevance;
6. transparent theoretical robustness rather than a large numerical apparatus.

The paper does **not** need to pretend to have an urban land/transport model (JUE), a new general fiscal-federalism theorem (JPubE), or an economic-geography conceptual contribution (JoEG). That is the decisive advantage.

The first-submission pitch should be:

> The paper identifies a strategic industrial-policy portfolio distortion: when multiple regional governments allocate fixed policy capacity across vertically complementary activities, incomplete jurisdictional capture produces a positive-measure interval in which duplicated high-return priorities are the unique Nash outcome even though coordinated reallocation of the same total capacity is welfare improving.

The pitch must immediately concede that spillover underinternalization, fixed budgets and policy composition are not individually new. The contribution is their interaction in a regional industrial-policy game and the exact configuration/threshold result.

## 7. Submission sequence

Canonical sequence:

1. **Journal of Regional Science** — primary submission.
2. **Regional Science and Urban Economics** — automatic first fallback if JRS rejects on novelty/ceiling/fit without identifying a correctness problem.
3. **Journal of Economic Geography (Economics section)** — alternative third route if the paper is re-presented around smart specialization, regional value chains and state/territorial policy, with no theory change and within the 8,000-word limit.
4. **The Annals of Regional Science** — safety net.

JUE is recorded as a stretch benchmark, not part of the default sequence. JPubE is excluded from the current sequence.

### Fallback logic

- If JRS rejects **for novelty magnitude / small model / insufficient breadth**: move to RSUE; do not add theory automatically.
- If JRS rejects **for absence of a spatial mechanism**: reconsider whether RSUE shares the same objection; JoEG may become a better next route if the contribution is valued as regional industrial-policy/value-chain theory.
- If JRS identifies **exact prior art or a mathematical flaw**: stop and reopen the relevant earlier workflow stage; do not simply resubmit downstream.
- If RSUE rejects for insufficient spatial structure but accepts the economic mechanism as correct: move to JoEG only after presentation-level economic-geography integration.
- If multiple strong-field journals agree that the contribution is correct but too narrow: move to The Annals of Regional Science rather than adding an unmotivated extension.
