## Selected architecture and why it differs from generic add-an-instrument exercise

**A1 full-game loop**: announced project-conditional grant `s_e` → private investor's exclusive partner `e` and viable project → local governments' ex ante x choices change because marginal sector benefit and tax change if chosen → feasible match quantity `m_e=x_upstream(1-x_downstream)` changes → investment selection and national real welfare change. In time order, grant is fixed first, portfolio choices anticipate investor's best response, investor picks last. Unlike a fixed network, partner identity changes endogenously and excludes alternative link production under the bottleneck.

**Nested benchmarks to recover**:
- `N0`: freeze project e exogenously → no investor switching; grants financed as specified; local portfolios still react.
- `N1`: freeze local x exogenously → investor link winner may change but no regional role switching.
- `N2`: fix grant s=0 → decentralized link and portfolio game only.
- `N3`: remove exclusive capacity (allow both links) → no single winner displacing an alternative.
- `N4`: enforce original 2-region fixed matching and grant off → original [THEORY_FREEZE](../../THEORY_FREEZE.md) when associated matching/benefit incidence parameters are restored. **Not a literal N=3 limit without removing investor and reallocating its share; must explicitly map parameters in Stage 4**.

**Distinct feedback hypothesis to attack:** implementability of a *real-welfare-maximizing joint regional role-and-project assignment* can fail even when a grant makes the privately preferred project active, because the grant can simultaneously divert the project from a more productive partner and change local portfolio incentives. A result about `the grant merely swaps project winners` is **not** enough for a standalone paper.

## B1 negative control: outside option makes coordination non-voluntary

Within the old two-region duplication wedge `Delta<A<Delta/(1-alpha)`, frozen government D assigned under `(U,D)` has `(1-alpha)A<Delta` (its duplicated outside payoff), so a symmetric unanimous governance institution **without** transfers, enforcement or changed incidence cannot get both governments to endorse the imposed asymmetric role choice. This illustrates why adding a committee is not an actual implementation mechanism. It is existing IR logic, not new theory.

## Why not choose A2/A4/B2 now

- A2 removes partner competition; individual links may be optimized independently and a grant can be treated as a simple subsidy on project activation.
- A4 adds mechanism complexity without identified new economic contribution, despite plausible grants/application competition.
- B2 reintroduces simple side payments whose generic implementation is already well studied.
