**Research status:** DISCOVERY / FRONTIER HYPOTHESIS REGISTER, **not** a Stage-2 novelty PASS, Stage-3 GO, Stage-4 authorization, or a revised manuscript.

Absence of a matching paper in this bounded search is **NOT** a proof of never-researched novelty.

## Executive answer

A cheap fixed compensation, a sequential contract, two-region specialization, a coalition, an information broker, and supply-chain resilience alone have already strong prior-art parents. The most credible **unresolved intersection** is *whether interregional coordination changes governments' executed industrial policy allocation from imitative to technologically complementary specialization*, holding apart (a) declared priorities, (b) own-budget spending, (c) jointly funded projects, and (d) real private production links. This is principally an **empirical/institutional causal question** and NOT proven absent from the literature.

The highest priority **pure theoretical** frontier to falsify is *the minimum jointly verifiable and legally enforceable public signal/instrument needed to turn a fixed-capacity role-specialization wedge into a voluntary intergovernmental agreement under strict cash-budget and policy-competence constraints*. But principal-agent, partial-verifiability, common-agency and budget-balanced mechanism design are very crowded parent classes; a threshold such as `monitoring precision × transfer budget > a known incentive gap` is trivial without an additional non-obvious equilibrium or welfare result.

A third conditional candidate is *static efficiency-improving regional specialization vs exposure to common/independent production shocks and policy-capacity constraints*, but supply-chain resilience/specialization theory already exists (Ferrari–Pesaresi 2025, Hardwick 2025, Todo 2026, IMF 2026); only a clearly intergovernmental contribution would count.

### Why prioritizing E1 and E2 is not a novelty certification

**E1 unique economic outcome target:** observable *executed regional policy allocation shares*, not documents announcing a sector, firms' patents, all-cohesion grant expenditure, project enrollment, or new collaborations alone. Paper must show changing regional OWN investment behavior distinct from automatic receipt/recording of I3 funding, and distinguish investment in same NACE sector **(mimicry)** from complementarity of *different production stages*. Even if no earlier exact study appears in this scan, closeness of P09–P11 and selection bias imply a **conditional not-verified gap**.

**E2 minimum candidate theorem:** a distribution-free, individually rational, commitment-feasible **policy-composition implementation region** under signed no-rival-contract rights and *observable legal outcome categories* that cannot be reduced to a known 2-player contracting theorem, Strîmbu's partial-verifiability all-pay formulation, Xue's public project budget mechanism or a basic `B≥lower bound` condition. Until one such nontrivial result is **formulated and killed against full parent theorems**, no Stage 4 authorization.

## C. The Oxford 2026 information-broker theorem-gap: mathematically cheap negative control

**Oxford TIDE WP96 (May 2026) is a central contemporary closest paper, not a novelty vacuum.** Its institutional vision aggregates each sovereign government's feasible best responses, returns aligned specialization scenarios and stresses iterative learning. It is primarily an institutional/coordinative optimization proposal; the inspected section does **not** independently certify truthful reporting or incentive compatibility in an arbitrary government payoff game.

With the **unchanged frozen complete-information local government model** and the strict duplication wedge `Delta<A<Delta/(1-alpha)`:

```text
d W_i / d x_i = Delta + alpha*A - A*x_j
               >= Delta - (1-alpha)*A > 0
for every x_j in [0,1].
```

Therefore an information broker who only sends **non-binding, costless recommendations and discloses nothing new about payoffs** cannot change the unique choice `x_i=1`: policy recommendation does not create any change in action incentive. This is an **immediate dominant-action implication of the old model**, not a novel theorem, and it does **not** refute the Oxford WP's richer dynamic/repeated negotiation framework. It concretely shows why any real E2 mechanism must possess a **new binding instrument, repeated-game enforceability, genuine learning of previously unknown states, or other verified incentive-relevant primitive**.
