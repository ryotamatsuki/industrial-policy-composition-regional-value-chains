## 3. First MODEL-SPECIFIC instrument companion: EXACT and already conceptually known
In the strict wedge suppose a credible EX ANTE ENFORCEABLE role-contingent interlocal transfer T>0 flows from U-beneficiary region 1 to D-switching region 2, only if roles (x_1,x_2)=(1,0) actually implemented. At baseline:
- Region 2 private opportunity loss: W_2(1,1)-W_2(0,1) = Delta−(1−alpha)A >0.
- Region 1 private cross-region production benefit: W_1(1,0)-W_1(1,1) = alpha*A >0.
- Pareto-improvement transfer interval: Delta−(1−alpha)A < T < alpha*A. Width: alpha A−[Delta−(1−alpha)A] = A−Delta >0. Checks policy-fund budget needs, actual cash incidence, enforceability and participation.
- The optimal TOTAL surplus is unchanged by a budget-balanced pure transfer, as intended; costs/enforcement/legality could shrink interval. Claiming a new general Coase/side-payment theorem here is INVALID. This is at most a **source-attributed POLICY COMPANION** to address editor's "what could be done." Transaction/administrative costs or a cap B lead simple feasibility inequalities and are NOT automatically new science.
- A nonbinding informational coordinator with no price/incentive change cannot overturn strict dominant U allocation in the wedge. Information alone is insufficient in this exact known game; information under REAL uncertainty/repeated learning is different model and has substantial prior theory.
- Symmetric direct reward s for choosing D (central earmarked grant, cost financed by lump-sum general resources exogenous to single-agent deviation) yields each region U vs D differential Delta+alpha A−A*x_j−s. Differentiated profiles (1,0)/(0,1) become Nash if Delta−(1−alpha)A < s < Delta+alpha A, but interior x_i=x_j=q_s=alpha+(Delta−s)/A also Nash (under unconstrained x∈[0,1]), i.e. **mere common subsidy removes unique duplication but may NOT guarantee unique optimal differentiation**. This is first-pass new MODEL DIAGNOSTIC, a straightforward algebraic policy result, not certified new economics; taxation, general equilibrium effects, grant conditionality, budget incidence and role selection need actual institutional modelling before policy recommendation. No claim all grants have same behavior.
