### How candidates differ and when to merge

- H01/H02/H03 are different **governance instruments**: bilateral voluntary contract, centralized grant rule, non-transfer collective decision. Do **not** assume their mechanisms are distinct without calculating equilibrium incentives later.
- H07 is an **asymmetric assignment/liquidity constraint**, not an instrument; it may be layered on H01/H02 only if it creates independent nontrivial strategic feedback. Otherwise merge H07 into its parent and retain its ID as `MERGED INTO ...`.
- H06 adds **endogenous cross-region link feasibility** (possibly spatial structure); H08 adds **private firm investment/location response**. They may become a single agent-network model if independent feedback cannot be separated; do not count that as two novel papers.
- H04 and H05 require private types or dynamic states, respectively, and should not be introduced as ornamental complexity.

## Stage 1/2 targeted falsification questions

1. **H01:** Is the claimed implementing transfer equilibrium a direct corollary of existing interjurisdictional transfer or endogenous side-payment results? Does IR/budget balance actually address contract **formation**, or only exogenous implementation?
2. **H02:** Does a central matching grant merely replicate a known Pigouvian correction? Are aggregate resources truly held fixed once financing and conditional grants are included?
3. **H03:** What decentralized voting/bargaining behavior differs analytically from giving the coordinator the planner's problem? Is a real government legally/institutionally bound by the collective rule?
4. **H06:** Does endogenous match formation change the strategic policy-composition result or simply replace constant A with an arbitrary increasing function?
5. **H07:** Do region-specific costs and liquidity produce a nontrivial assignment/incentive result or merely a mechanical feasibility inequality? Are liquidity and policy capacity separate resources?
