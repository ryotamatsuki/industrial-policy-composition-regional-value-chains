## 1. Answer to the frontier question

There are **three independently mature lines of research** that strongly absorb the proposed H01 innovation:

1. **Regional public economics:** voluntary horizontal fiscal transfers with the timing of transfers vs policies are already studied in **Hindriks & Myles (2003)**; bargaining over local equalization funds and higher-level preset schemes in **Di Liddo (2017)**; complementary *two-district* public inputs and voluntary contributions to a foreign region in **Gregor & Šťastná (2012)**. These are closer than generic federal grants alone.
2. **Endogenous game modification:** **Jackson & Wilkie (2005)** already establish strategic incentive failures under unbounded preplay action-contingent transfers; the [2013 correction](https://web.stanford.edu/~jacksonm/endog-erratum.pdf) invalidates their Theorem 5 (3+ players), **not Theorem 2** (two-player supportability). **Gregor (2015)** analyzes complementary *role/task division* with cash gifts—particularly close to our U/D division narrative.
3. **2025–2026 frontier:** **Geffner–Oesterheld–Conitzer (2025)** prove constrained multiround unanimous-transfer protocols can implement welfare-improving profiles in finite games; **Heitzig (2025)** derives a conditional commitment mechanism implementing public-goods cooperative core via strong NE; **Kavner (IJCAI 2026)** proves efficient feasible transfer equilibria with consensus/veto rules; **Liu–Farina–Ozdaglar (EC 2026)** obtain self-enforcing/internal-transfer equilibrium and a mediated implementation result for finite games. Thus **making transfers credible/conditional, using veto, or adding a mediator is not in itself a new contribution**.

**Economic interpretation:** The original two-region *fixed policy-capacity* allocation and income incidence are specific and the identities are correct, but a model-specific compensation interval and a proposer–acceptor game are elementary instances of heavily studied mechanisms. A JRS-style editorial objection can be partly *answered* by using known economics with correct attribution; this does **not** turn the application into new high-field-journal contract theory.

## 4. Explicit correction to earlier H01 pilot (critical)

### Correct part

The contracted portfolio game **with one exogenously binding specific schedule** `T=t x_1(1-x_2)`, `L=Delta-(1-alpha)A`, `U=alpha*A` has unique coordinated policy Nash for `L<t≤U`, and both regions gain strictly over original duplicate at `L<t<U`; total real welfare rises by `A-Delta`. With a designated proposer, receiver-veto, an **exclusive finite menu** and exact instance `Delta=1,A=3/2,alpha=1/2`, the `0.05`-increment example has accepted `t=0.30` and payoff gains `(0.45,0.05)`. That is a *particular protocol*, not endogenous free contracting.

### Overstatement to retract/qualify

The previous report [H01_VOLUNTARY_CONTRACT_CHALLENGE.md](H01_VOLUNTARY_CONTRACT_CHALLENGE.md) framed "no pure supported outcome in unrestricted, simultaneous continuous game via Jackson–Wilkie Theorem 2" as a result. It used the correct finite two-player solo-payoff logic, but **failed to verify** all assumptions for the continuum action space and the **entire unbounded space of discontinuous side-payment function offers**.

The original author working draft, Appendix "Games with Continuum Actions", **explicitly cautions** that arbitrary discontinuous offers can yield continuation subgames **without Nash equilibria**. To make sequential equilibrium/supportability statements, it instead restricts admissible transfer functions to a suitable class of *continuous* functions with additional compact/convex structure ensuring second-stage equilibria and gives a suitably modified theorem. This is a genuine missing construction, not a cosmetic citation fix.

Correct classification:
- **Finite corner-restricted second-stage game** `x_i∈{0,1}`, arbitrary transfer offers: applying Jackson–Wilkie Theorem 2 gives a valid model-specific no-pure-supportability illustration (plus the solo-payoff proof).
- **Original continuous portfolio** `x_i∈[0,1]` with one designated proposer and restricted ex ante signed `T`: G2 concrete finite-menu SPNE test holds.
- **Original continuous portfolio with unrestricted simultaneous arbitrary transfer functions**: `UNRESOLVED / NOT WELL-POSED FOR GLOBAL SPNE UNTIL ADMISSIBLE FUNCTION SPACE AND EVERY OFF-PATH CONTINUATION ARE SPECIFIED`; cannot invoke Theorem 2 unconditionally.

This correction must be carried into every future paper/discussion. Do not merge finite and continuous strategy spaces into a single theorem.

### Erratum precision

The [2013 official erratum](https://doi.org/10.1093/restud/rds039) says original **Theorem 5 (three or more players) is false**, other theorems remain valid, and the status of Corollaries 1/2 is unclear. Thus Theorem 2 stays valid **within its own assumptions**; the erratum's assurance does not imply that every different infinite-dimensional contracting protocol qualifies.

## 5. What theoretical avenues, if any, remain a frontier?

**No certified general-mechanism gap found.** At most three *targeted research QUESTIONS*, none automatically authorized as a new model, remain conceivable.

**Q-A: enforceable partial contractibility in actual local governments.** In a specific horizontal regional contract, can governments credibly commit a measurable **mix of investments** or only project-specific cost shares? What happens if `x_i` is an administrative priority index, not verifiable spending or output? What changes when the contract's objective is measurable but the underling opportunity-cost/sector allocation is not? **Risk**: standard incomplete contracting/hidden action, not novel just because labelled regional.

**Q-B: fiscal legality + participation and renegotiation.** Do annual appropriation, multi-year commitments, project auditing, and region-level budget headroom create an endogenous institution-selection problem that changes efficient specialization? **Risk**: common fiscal federalism / liquidity-limited contracting; no observed source-backed legal/contractible primitive yet.

**Q-C: identified empirical institutional effects of real horizontal coordination.** Can actual regional intergovernmental cooperation rules be associated with changes in **comparable sector-priority budget shares** and cross-region input-output links? This needs an original *data feasibility* and counterfactual identification design addressing interference; potential is empirical, **not an application of H01 algebra alone**.

**Source-evidence-first order for any Q-A/B model:** (i) identify a published actual agreement/legal framework, (ii) determine the *real* authority to move money, project indicators, party membership/veto, funding appropriation and monitoring, (iii) decide measurable economic controls, and (iv) only then write a strategic game and exact parent comparison. An arbitrary hypothetical `T` does not license an institutional policy recommendation.
