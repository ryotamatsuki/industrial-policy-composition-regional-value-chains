## 4. Mechanism-independent algebraic NEGATIVE CONTROL — explicitly not novel

Assume independent, risk-neutral decision units in a one-project, one-period game. National authority has already pledged a firm subsidy of cost `s≥0` (this commitment is EXOGENOUS, not equilibrium). Municipality needs irreversible infrastructure `K≥0`. A given firm operates with exogenous chance `p∈[0,1]`. If operating, residents get `B` and rest of nation gets `S`; if it FAILS, `r∈[0,1]` fraction of K is salvaged. Negative S allowed to represent displaced national activity. A lump-sum grant `t∈R` is signed (t<0 means LOCAL pays national) and enforceable before construction. Not an endogenous company location, exit, debt, incomplete-contracting, bank loan, insurance or political choice model.

The expected unrecovered capital cost is

```math
C = K[1-(1-p)r].
L_0=pB-C,\quad N_0=pS-s,\quad W=L_0+N_0.
L(t)=L_0+t,\quad N(t)=N_0-t.
```

Each tier voluntarily AGREES iff

```math
C-pB \le t \le pS-s.
```

This interval exists **if and only if** `W=p(B+S)-C-s ≥ 0`. The equivalence is the ELEMENTARY transferable-utility compensation condition (Coase / standard grant transfer arithmetic). It is **NOT a new implementation theorem**. If only t≥0 (national pays local, not vice versa), the condition becomes `max(0,C-pB) ≤ pS-s`, a trivial finance sign restriction.

**Exact, certified negative-control cases** (computed with Python `fractions.Fraction`):
- `p=4/5,B=4,S=5,K=5,r=1/2,s=1`: `C=9/2`; local without transfer `-13/10` so municipality refuses, total welfare `+17/10` so nationally worthwhile; admissible national→local signed transfer `t∈[13/10,3]`. A Pigouvian/Coasian compensation solves refusal.
- `p=4/5,B=8,S=0,K=5,r=1/2,s=3`: local without transfer `+19/10`, social `-11/10`, precommitted national subsidy cost makes local public investment privately desirable but aggregate bad. NOT an equilibrium of benevolent national subsidy-setting; national at the initial stage would reject if it internalized costs.
- `p=2/5,B=3,S=3,K=6,r=0,s=1`: local `-24/5`, national before transfer `+1/5`, total `-23/5`; no mutually beneficial transfer.
- Exact rational 1296-point parameter grid verifies equivalence signed feasible ↔ `W≥0`; 1296 finite cases DO NOT prove it for all parameters; the preceding algebra does.

Source code `revision/post-jrs/code/fiscal_risk_transfer_negative_control.py` implements examples and grid. Any published argument stopping at `C-pB` and `pS-s` threshold is **merely classic cost-sharing** and is a strict NO-GO as new economic-theory paper.

### What exactly the toy proves and does NOT prove

- Demonstrates the **NAIVE** hypothesis 'local decline despite good national industry project' does not require firm hold-up or new institutional theorem — immediate transfer arithmetic.
- Demonstrates 'local wishes to overinvest in bad national project' likewise arises in trivial exogenously subsidized settings; a real equilibrium must account for WHY national authority precommits to s despite losing money.
- Demonstrates how firm failure/salvage enters expected fiscal exposure mechanically. NO theorem about strategic EXIT, moral hazard, creditor screening, time consistency, stochastic aggregate risk, heterogeneous regions, fiscal rules, or national rationality is implied.
