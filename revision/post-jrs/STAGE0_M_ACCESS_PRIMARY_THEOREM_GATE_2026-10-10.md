## C. Direct negative-control derivation (NOT a novel proposition)

One firm can apply to programs 1 and 2. Conditional net benefit of each after its per-application expense is `v_i=b_i-c_i`. A fixed dossier/setup cost `F>0` applies **once** across all simultaneously requested programs; assume no budget scarcity or rival applications. If either program must be accessed ALONE, then no take-up when `v_1<F` and `v_2<F`. If permitted to stack and the application cost is shared, take BOTH whenever `v_1+v_2>F`. Thus the open region

```math
\max(v_1,v_2)<F<v_1+v_2
```

supports no *individual* application but positive *joint* take-up. Introducing exclusivity closes this possibility. A central application intermediary that removes F has obvious participation effects; none is a new game-theoretic result, and the intuition is directly anticipated by Kleven–Kopczuk, Honda et al, Kleinman, and innovation grant application economics. If a government unilaterally raises program generosity or reduces F, sign effects on *actual innovation output* cannot be claimed without production, selection and government budget opportunity costs.
