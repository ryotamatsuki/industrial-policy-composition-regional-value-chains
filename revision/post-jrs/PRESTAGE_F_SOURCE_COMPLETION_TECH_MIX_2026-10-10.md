## 2. F-TECH original theorem passports: endogenous technology adoption is a CROWDED frontier

### TECH-P1 — Alvarez, Buera & Trachter (2026) [P-MODEL/PROP]
**Technology Adoption and Optimal Policy**, Richmond Fed WP 26-09 (67pp version May 6, 2026); DOI https://doi.org/10.21144/wp26-09 , original PDF https://www.richmondfed.org/-/media/RichmondFedOrg/publications/research/working_papers/2026/wp26-09.pdf ; distinct 88pp NBER WP 35133 version https://www.nber.org/papers/w35133 , versions NOT silently interchangeable.

- **Players and timing:** monopolistically competitive heterogeneous technology-gap firms; fixed-cost adoption of moving frontier; dynamic representative consumer/planner; one national policy setter; not a competitive game of multiple local governments.
- **Prop. 6 (printed p.21):** time-invariant static/input subsidy and dynamic/adoption subsidy decentralize an allocation satisfying planner necessary conditions as an equilibrium. **Does not guarantee UNIQUE optimal allocation** when nonconcavities permit alternative equilibria.
- **Prop. 16 (p.36):** aggregate technology/adoption complementarity parameter `ζ≤1` (concave aggregation) implies UNIQUE dynamic equilibrium and efficient implementation with optimal policy. Props. 17–20 supply other unique-implementation regimes; **Props. 21–22 (pp.39–40)** show poverty and **abundance** traps with suboptimal equilibria even at optimal subsidies.
- **Conspicuous limitation directly stated in original pp.6–7:** production has a stylized **symmetric roundabout IO** technology (no differential sector centrality); firms ex ante identical apart from technology gaps; effectively single national planner/policy instrument set. Original authors CONJECTURE qualitative policy remains, but dynamic implementation harder under heterogeneity. **This is a model limitation, not an open-theorem certificate.**
- **Direct absorption:** 'tech adoption endogenous', 'two types of optimal subsidies', 'existence or uniqueness under concavity', 'adoption poverty trap' are already results. Merely tacking on geography is NOT a stand-alone theorem.

### TECH-P2 — Buera & Trachter (2024) [P-MODEL, local analytical results]
**Sectoral Development Multipliers**, Richmond Fed WP 24-02 (55pp), DOI https://doi.org/10.21144/wp24-02 , original PDF https://www.richmondfed.org/-/media/RichmondFedOrg/publications/research/working_papers/2024/wp24-02.pdf .
- **Model (printed pp.3–5):** heterogeneous firms choose traditional/modern technology in a **MULTISECTOR network**; different production matrix `Ω=[ω_ss']` and investment/adoption goods matrix `Λ=[λ_ss']`; govt chooses sector-specific revenue, labor, intermediate input, and technology adoption subsidies.
- **Main analytical object:** sector-specific subsidy effect divided by fiscal cost, decomposed into production efficiency and endogenous adoption, involving a **double-Leontief inverse** (main intro/results). Quantitatively tech adoption can REVERSE rank of target sectors; adoption subsidies are more cost effective in investigated calibration.
- **Source-verified conceptual difference to TECH-P1:** heterogeneous sectors/IO network **already** modeled in 2024; dynamic equilibrium implementation along entire technology-transition paths and local-government strategic competition are not the comparative-statics object of its inspected sections.
- **HARD threat:** An extension of TECH-P1 to heterogeneous network centrality cannot claim novelty from 'adding sector IO links' alone; that structure exists in TECH-P2. Novelty, if any, would have to be a truly new source-unabsorbed **dynamic equilibrium/implementation or competition result**, not a static 'network centrality determines policy' re-derivation.

### TECH-P3 — Liu & Ma (2023 revised original WP) [A]
**Innovation Networks and R&D Allocation**, https://www.nber.org/papers/w29607 . Cross-sector innovation network, optimal R&D policy allocation and growth spillovers already formalized; planner targets sectors central to knowledge creation, accounting for international spillovers. This is **innovation**, distinct from tech adoption, but directly absorbs 'sector-network centrality drives subsidies' as headline. Proposition/proof comparison pending.

### TECH-P4 — IMF (2024) [M]
**Industrial Policies for Innovation: A Cost-Benefit Framework** https://www.elibrary.imf.org/view/journals/001/2024/176/article-A001-en.xml ; builds Liu–Ma endogenous innovation network and implements targeted-vs-horizontal policies with **imperfect implementation capacity/political targeting errors**; numerical welfare analysis: sector targeting best only under restrictive conditions. Captures 'administrative capability makes subsidy mix second best', not necessarily a published general analytical theorem.

### TECH-P5 — Chung, Ahn, Lee & Kim (2024) [A publisher abstract]
**Optimal Subsidy Policy for Innovation: Technology Push and Demand Pull**, https://doi.org/10.1177/10591478241231848 . Government commits to a **policy MIX** (technology-push R&D reward to manufacturers, demand-pull subsidy to consumers), competing firms choose R&D then market behavior under spillovers. Optimal regime changes from no support to push only to both as social benefits increase. Thus a TWO-INSTRUMENT substitution/complementarity **threshold** is known outside the locality topic. Full proof not checked.

**TECH preliminary scholarly verdict:** THEORY-GENERALIZATION CANDIDATE remains possible ONLY as an explicitly new theorem (e.g. a sharp dynamic unique-implementation boundary with nontrivial heterogeneous IO and decentralized government choices) whose proof is NOT an immediate corollary of P1 + P2 + P3, not 'technology adoption'. Topic fit may be DIRECT/ADJACENT or, if purely mathematical, OUTSIDE and independently ROUTED. **No theorem proposed that currently survives parent absorption**. No mathematical NO-GO based on policy irrelevance.

## 3. F-MIX original Japanese source cluster: policy availability ≠ actual uptake

### MIX-E1 — Okamuro & Nishimura (2026), HIAS-E-158 [E/M original full 22pp]
**Complementarity between Hard and Soft Support? The Effects of Place-based R&D Policy Instrument Mix on Local Innovation**
https://hias.hit-u.ac.jp/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/HIAS-E-158.pdf
- Original **§5 pp.12–13, Table 5**: 146 municipalities, 18 years. Fixed effects show financing support and nonfinancial services related to innovation/productivity differently; **system GMM** finds **positive estimated interaction** for hard × soft, and only soft has independently significant main coefficients in these specifications. Measures often count **number/types of available instruments**, not actual annual financing amount.
- Original **§6 limitations p.14**: sample of municipalities already providing support (no comparable untreated no-program localities), imperfect administrative histories, not fine details/amounts of each measure; hence NOT sufficient to deduce causally robust optimal budgetary shares or true nationwide innovation additionality.

### MIX-E2 — Nishimura & Okamuro (2026), HIAS-E-159 [E/M original full 38pp]
**How should place-based R&D support be designed and implemented? Evidence from a firm survey in Japan**
https://hias.hit-u.ac.jp/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/HIAS-E-159.pdf
- Original **§6–7 printed pp.21–23**: firm survey, multilevel national/prefecture/city × finance vs advisory/support, and government vs local private supporters. Positive **city+local supporter** complementarity; NO statistically significant multilevel govt or within-government hard × soft complementarity in THIS firm-level specification.
- **Interviews p.21**: subsidy programs sometimes **mutually exclusive** between city and national/prefectural providers; weak government-to-government information sharing; firms unaware of policy packages, blocking joint take-up. Authors themselves propose information exchange and support ecosystem improvements. **This is an observed institutional issue**, not proof of an unstudied theorem.
- Cannot call E1 and E2 'inconsistent estimates of one quantity' or select whichever favors our hypothesis: they measure different units, outcomes, policy availability vs participation and use different empirical specifications.

### MIX-E3 — Okamuro & Nishimura (2026), HIAS-E-157 [E/M full 25pp]
**The effects of place-based startup policy mix on local entrepreneurship**
https://hias.hit-u.ac.jp/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/HIAS-E-157.pdf
- **§6 pp.11–16**: City government survey; grants/seminars positive for number of new firms but subsidies negatively associated with **AVERAGE FIRM SIZE**; local counts and quality/employment outcomes can diverge. Hard × soft subsidy-seminar interaction not significant in the inspected specification. Their explanation of low-quality entry is a plausible hypothesis, NOT separately identified displaced national entrepreneurship or proven welfare effect.

### MIX-P4 — Forslid & Okubo (2024), RIETI 24-E-015 [P-MODEL/M]
https://www.rieti.go.jp/jp/publications/dp/24e015.pdf
- International heterogeneous multi-product firm setting with product-development subsidies, fiscal tax incidence and relocation; government **§2.8 printed p.15** shows **SIMULATED Nash reaction functions** under partial agglomeration, not a general symbolic Nash uniqueness/existence theorem. Already includes subsidy competition and product-scope margin.
- Plain 'noncooperative subsidy decision and regional firm relocation' is existing theory; don't confuse Nash SIMULATION with an analytic theorem.

### MIX-P5 — Kondo (2013) JIE [A published publisher abstract]
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jinteco.2012.04.004
- R&D subsidy competition, endogenous growth, geography, vertical IO; higher vs lower trade costs change intensity of subsidy competition and economic growth. Earlier repo confirms a cooperation/transfer section. The **PUBLISHED exact formal propositions remain source-blocked**; cannot certify any new equilibrium regime before obtaining.

### MIX-P6 — Agrawal, Hoyt & Wilson (2022), JEL survey [M/A official]
https://doi.org/10.1257/jel.20201490
- Local fiscal policy CHOICE synthesis across many mechanisms and multiple fiscal instruments; higher-level restrictions and mobility already inside the surveyed framework. Novelty cannot be established by municipal terminology, multi-instrument label or second tier of public authority.

### MIX-P7 — Ogawa & Wildasin (2009), AER [A official]
https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.99.4.1206
- Under specific markets/spillback conditions, decentralized governments' policy games can be **globally efficient without Pigouvian higher-government correction**. Therefore 'any regional spillover necessarily entails decentralization inefficiency' is **FALSE as a universal theorem**. Must identify conditions for competition to help or hurt.

## 5. Consequential frontier correction

2025 Lashkaripour–Wu's review describes technology-choice industrial policy as relatively underdeveloped, but **the 2024 Buera–Trachter multi-sector and 2026 Alvarez–Buera–Trachter dynamic adoption papers already close large parts of that gap**. The question is not 'which new subsidies induce technology adoption?', nor 'which network-central sectors to support?', nor 'do two subsidies internalize dynamic adoption?'. Identify only a **source-unabsorbed theorem**. If none, STOP this extension as a paper while keeping general math theory relevance independently assessed.

Japanese 2026 HIAS studies provide an unusually concrete **institutional** counterweight: policy menus measured by local govts can exhibit estimated hard-soft complementarities, but actual *firm-level co-use* and multilevel complementation may fail where information/eligibility is fragmented. An effective theory question must generate behavior, not assume automatic take-up. HOWEVER existing soft-support/matching/coordination literature may have already explained this; require nearest original mechanism theorem comparison.
