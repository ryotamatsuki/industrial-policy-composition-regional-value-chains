## 2. Phenomenon versus explanation

**Observed source-grounded policy landscape (not a causal finding of duplication):**

1. The [OECD 2025 place-based industrial-policy analysis](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/place-based-industrial-policy_43edc0df-en.html) emphasizes alignment of national sector priorities, heterogeneous local capabilities and coordination across government levels.
2. The [OECD policy paper published 2026-10-09](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/targeting-places-in-national-industrial-strategies_284fcc80-en.html) proposes place diagnostics and central-local co-design of what each location can credibly do, how it connects to other regions/sectors, and what policy lever to use.
3. The European Commission's [I3-2026 Strand 1 call](https://eismea.ec.europa.eu/funding-opportunities/calls-proposals/interregional-innovation-investments-strand-1-i3-2026-inv1_en) explicitly funds interregional consortia around shared or complementary Smart Specialisation priorities, with financial and advisory support.
4. The [I3 Observatory 2026 report announcement](https://interregional-innovation-investments.ec.europa.eu/news/new-publication-i3-instrument-observatory-report-2026) describes value-chain and continuing-cooperation outcomes for I3 projects. These reported outputs **do not** identify a counterfactual coordination failure or estimate parameters of our game.

**Verified theoretical puzzle in the original model:** the certified fixed-capacity two-region game can yield a *unique duplicated priority* despite a planner-preferred alternative allocation. This is a mathematical result within its assumptions, **not evidence that actual jurisdictions frequently duplicate policies inefficiently**.

**Explanatory hypotheses, explicitly unverified:** regions may underinvest in complementary roles because of (a) uncompensated gains elsewhere; (b) third-party co-financing or budget frictions; (c) joint-decision/veto rules; (d) private information; (e) commitment and sunk costs; (f) missing cross-regional production partners; or (g) private firm response. Which mechanism is substantively new is open.

**Problem diagnosed in editorial feedback:** a constrained planner optimum says which portfolio is desirable within the model, not what feasible agreement, incentive, fiscal instrument, governance rule or private-sector response makes the outcome implementable.

| Dimension | Certified baseline | Candidate revision margins — NOT adopted |

## 4. Eight structurally distinct candidate mechanisms

These are **Stage-0 hypotheses**, not mathematically solved, novel, or publication-ready results. IDs are stable and detailed in [candidate register](STAGE0_CANDIDATE_REGISTER.md).

| ID | Distinct strategic problem | Plausible new economic question | Stage 0 disposition |
|---|---|---|---|
| H01 Conditional bilateral compensation | Local governments bargain/commit to contingent interregional payments before portfolio choice | Can mutually beneficial compensation emerge voluntarily and uniquely implement specialization? | **AUDIT** as minimal intervention benchmark; exceptionally high classic-theorem overlap risk |
| H02 Central conditional co-financing | National/regional funding authority chooses grant rules under a financing envelope; regions then choose portfolios | When do conditional grants correct composition without simply expanding aggregate subsidy spending? | **AUDIT** as real-world instrument branch |
| H03 Joint-governance/consortium rules | Multiple governments choose common priorities through agenda, voting, veto or binding role agreements without bilateral cash payments | When can collective decision rights implement differentiated priorities, and when does veto-induced gridlock dominate? | **AUDIT** as non-transfer institutional counterfactual |
| H04 Hidden regional capability/cost | Regions privately observe conversion costs/capabilities and have incentives to misreport to coordinator | Which assignments/instruments remain incentive-compatible under incomplete information? | **DEFER**, high mechanism-design overlap and verification cost |
| H05 Irreversibility/commitment over time | Regions make sequential or sunk sectoral investments, creating interregional hold-up | Can an early agreement prevent ex-post lock-in, and when does commitment worsen outcomes? | **DEFER**, new dynamic-game theory would be substantial |
| H06 Endogenous interregional matching | Regions/projects choose partners and complementary links, changing feasible match/output network | When does coordination change the network itself, not merely the split of existing fixed matches? | **AUDIT**, spatial/value-chain bridge with high general-network-game risk |
| H07 Heterogeneous roles and fiscal feasibility | Regions have different conversion costs, fiscal capacity and feasible transfer roles | Does the socially efficient assignment differ from implementable regional specialization, and can central support restore it? | **AUDIT**, conditional linkage to H01/H02 to be checked for redundancy |
| H08 Firm investment and crowd-in | Firms respond to policy portfolios by choosing productive investment/industry location/participation | Does private-sector response change the sign, ordering or size of the priority-duplication wedge? | **DEFER** pending evidence; may alter the original mechanism substantially |

**Fingerprint separation:** H01 changes *contracting*, H02 adds *central policymaker/financing*, H03 changes *collective decision rights*, H04 changes *information*, H05 changes *time/irreversibility*, H06 changes *network feasibility*, H07 changes *asymmetric roles/liquidity*, H08 changes *private actor reactions*. H07 could collapse into H01/H02; Stage 2 must test and merge if it generates no independent game. H03 could be a simple cooperative-planner restatement; this is a specific kill test.

## 5. Strongest already visible prior-art threats (NOT a completed novelty audit)

- **Fixed-budget allocation is known:** Kosec & Mogues, *Public Investment Choices by Local and Central Governments*, [World Bank Economic Review (2020)](https://academic.oup.com/wber/article/34/Supplement_1/S52/5645046).
- **Interregional implementation transfers are known:** Pfingsten & Wagener, [*Centralized vs. Decentralized Redistribution: A Case for Interregional Transfer Mechanisms* (1997)](https://ideas.repec.org/a/kap/itaxpf/v4y1997i4p429-451.html); budget-balanced side payments alone cannot establish novelty.
- **Federal grants/transfer and strategic timing are known:** Armbruster & Hintermann, [*Decentralization with porous borders* (2020)](https://edoc.unibas.ch/entities/publication/63a17677-82fa-401d-b45e-04506fbef9a3).
- **Broader parent classes to test:** local-public-good provision, public-expenditure composition, mechanism design/participation, assignment games/cooperative bargaining, political economy of voting/veto, dynamic hold-up, network formation/matching, spatial industrial subsidy competition and endogenous firm location.

Stage 2 must read original model/propositions and ask whether any candidate result is a **direct specialization of a known theorem**. Having a different application label or instrument name is insufficient. These initial references are a frontier map, **not a PASS**.

## 6. Evidence that would make this an important regional-industrial-policy problem

Stage 1/2 should distinguish normative theoretical predictions from observable institutional facts. Material evidence includes:

- Comparable regional priorities and grants *before* a coordinated program versus observed post-program priority changes; carefully separate strategic duplication from correlated comparative advantages.
- Cross-regional supplier-buyer/project links and their gains, plus which regional actors actually capture those gains (ownership and tax incidence matter).
- Actual co-financing, consortium rulebooks, partner-formation procedures, and compliance/enforcement conditions (I3 is **not** a bilateral local-government transfer contract).
- Regional conversion costs/capabilities, budgets and liquidity; check whether capacity, cash, grants and social benefits can be separately measured.
- Credible counterfactual, identification, or calibration plan, if an empirical extension is later authorized. A stylized anecdote is not causal validation.

At Stage 0 the evidence above is an **acquisition plan**, not a claim that these datasets are currently accessible or identify causality.

## 7. Provisional research route and question

**Route:** mechanism-first theoretical revision with a bounded institutional-evidence bridge. An empirical or mixed path remains an alternative only if credible data and identification can be established at Stage 1/2; do not require data as a universal gate.

**Single falsifiable research question:**

> Under what institutional and resource constraints can coordination of fixed-capacity regional industrial-policy portfolios implement welfare-improving cross-regional value-chain complementarity, and when does implementation fail despite a planner-preferred reallocation?

"Implement" must later be defined through actual game timing, instruments, feasible strategies, information and equilibrium concepts. It is falsifiable by a closest-theorem absorption, impossibility of a feasible instrument, or counterexample to a claimed universal condition. This is an **intake question**, not a certified theorem.

**Editorial-value hypothesis:** The paper could distinguish *why* portfolio duplication persists from *which feasible regional/national governance choices* actually change sectoral roles and welfare. This is worthwhile **only if** a non-obvious condition or impossibility survives Stage 2 novelty and Stage 4A mathematical attack.

**Skeptical editor's preliminary rejection:** The anticipated results may simply relabel standard fiscal-federalism grants, transfers, bargaining, or matching propositions as sectoral industrial policy. Funding or new regional heterogeneity might trivially tighten a participation constraint without producing an independent strategic contribution. Genuine institutional program descriptions do not validate welfare or the alleged inefficiency. If so, a narrower diagnostic note or different research question is more defensible than a larger but derivative model.
