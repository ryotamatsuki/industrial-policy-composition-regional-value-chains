## 1. Executive decision

The original JRS editor asked what practically *should* and *could* internalize the otherwise unexplained interregional policy-composition externality. Our Stage-4 A1 experiment failed because (i) zero intervention already implemented the optimum and (ii) the entire subsidy-diversion welfare loss survived with local policy shares fixed.

A fresh literature-informed Stage-3 search explored genuinely different strategic dimensions: **local private information (H04), sequential/irreversible decisions (H05), and endogenous private firm actions (H08)**. All were subjected to an application-neutral parent-class screen; ten structured candidates, below, are consolidated/deduplicated. The search finds **no proposal yet supported by a novel full-game-only candidate result** strong enough to justify the bounded Stage-4 investment. In particular:

- Asymmetric-information **public-works grant contracting** is well established; attaching region-specific sector-premium types without a new incentive/welfare result is weak.
- Multi-region **learning and experimentation** including industrial targeting and regional spillovers are deeply developed parent classes.
- Local-government **competition for mobile firms**, along with public input subsidies and endogenous location, is established in theoretical and quantitative work.
- **Investment timing/hold-up** is established, and a particularly cheap change—making the original region-government portfolio choices sequential instead of simultaneous—**provably fails to remedy duplication in the original wedge** (Section 4).
- Any combination of these components could *in principle* produce new strategic feedback. Their separate presence in earlier papers is **not proof** that all prospective combined games are exact prior art. Here, however, **we do not have a specific testable nontrivial full-game-only result, credible implementation mapping, and bounded primitive list** for a GO decision.

**Disposition `NO-GO` is therefore a project-selection/falsification judgment under the preannounced Stage-3 gate**, not an exact-prior-art claim for every conceivable future model. Do not evade it by promising “one more parameter.”

## 4. Exact negative result: sequential timing does not fix original wedge

**Inputs from frozen two-region model**:

```text
W_i(x_i,x_j) = b_D + Delta*x_i
             + alpha*A*x_i*(1-x_j)
             + (1-alpha)*A*(1-x_i)*x_j,
Delta>0, A>0, alpha∈(0,1), x_i∈[0,1].
```

Keep the entire original payoff and information fixed, but change move order: region 1 (leader) commits x1; region 2 (follower) observes it and chooses x2.

**In the original strict welfare-wedge region `Delta<A<Delta/(1-alpha)`:**

```text
dW2/dx2 = Delta + alpha*A - A*x1
        >= Delta - (1-alpha)*A > 0   for all x1∈[0,1].
```

Thus the follower's unique optimal action **at every history** is `x2=1`. The leader anticipates this and chooses its optimal `x1` against x2=1:

```text
dW1(x1,1)/dx1 = Delta-(1-alpha)*A > 0.
```

Therefore the **unique subgame-perfect outcome** is `(x1,x2)=(1,1)`, whereas the original fixed-capacity planner strictly prefers `(1,0)` or `(0,1)` with welfare gain `A-Delta>0`.

This is a **narrow exact timing-invariance statement**, not a theorem about irreversible investments with sunk adjustment costs or ex post bargaining. At the boundary `A=Delta/(1-alpha)` there are indifferences and the strict conclusion cannot simply be extended. The symmetric game with region 2 moving first gives the same strict-wedge conclusion.

[Symbolic checking code](verify_stage3_reentry_timing.py) directly reconstructs slopes from the original payoff, verifies the lower bound algebraically, and tests rational parameter examples. Source analytic argument, not numerical examples, justifies the whole strict wedge. Existing Lean scope is not extended by this diagnostic.
