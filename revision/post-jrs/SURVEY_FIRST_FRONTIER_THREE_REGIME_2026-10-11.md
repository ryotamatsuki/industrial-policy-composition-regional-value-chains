## E. Research Delta register after survey-first hostile comparison

### F-RISK (tentative priority to TEST, not claimed virgin)
**Question:** When NATIONAL authorities subsidize mobile strategic-sector firms, LOCAL authorities are expected to sink nonrecoverable infrastructure/housing/human-capital outlays and assume debt while national tax/security returns and project failure risks are asymmetrically borne, what mechanism determines whether local authorities should **opt out** even though the national industrial policy is worthwhile ex ante? Can an implementable national-local matching/insurance rule change equilibria and welfare if government cannot commit to ex post bailouts?

- Original phenomenon **2026 Sweden case**, OECD 2025 and Japan mission-oriented cases; economic significance high and DIRECT relevance.
- Closest source threats: old local central co-finance political-economy, intergovernmental grants Huber/Runkel 2006/2008, Janeba/Wilson 2011, 2026 Dai private-info budget, national industrial siting and environmental liability sharing, repeated soft-budget federalism (Halac/Yared). **NOT YET fully compared.**
- One binding economic DIFFERENCE to screen: **decision and contingent downside liability across separate tiers** with complementary national firm subsidy and municipally funded sunk input. Mere costs C and benefit b, or adding default probability p to a subsidy, is **TRIVIAL** and cannot be a publishable theorem.
- Required novel payoff mechanism conditional: contractibility of local co-investment relative to nationally granted subsidy, endogenous firm siting & exit and alternative local projects, social vs municipal risk incidence; needs a nonobvious equilibrium or new implementability condition beyond common-agency matching grants. Nothing proven yet.
- STATUS **FRONTIER HYPOTHESIS / SOURCE BLOCKED** not research-paper GO.

### F-VH (vertical constraints × horizontal local subsidy competition with multiple instruments)
**Question:** With simultaneously upper-level restrictions on LOCAL policy bundles and HORIZONTAL spillovers/strategic interactions, can a sharp comparative-static sign of changing central control differ from established partial decentralization and 2026 spatial industrial subsidy Nash? Distinguish fiscal requirements, local infrastructure and actual nationally controlled incentives; incorporate at most ONE realistic friction to avoid artificial complexity.

- Source explicitly motivates the **INTERSECTION** in JEL2022 Conclusion pp84–86; 2026 Dai addresses asymmetric-info budget-institution subset, 2026 Lin/Li addresses subsidy competition and trade costs; none author-certified universal gap.
- Originality threat extreme: 2004 multi-principal contracts, 2018 subsidy instruments, 2011 Janeba/Wilson, matching grants etc.
- **Status: CONDITIONAL source-based candidate, NOT proved unaddressed**.
- Finite search: cross citation 2023–Oct2026 joint restrictions and horizontal firm-entry strategic games; if one published parent characterizes the desired wedge STOP.

### F-EVAL (narrow empirical open problem, potentially most feasible)
**Question:** Can one empirically distinguish **local gross grant spending**, **local contingent public co-investment and debt liabilities**, **private investment that would have occurred anyway**, and **national net additionality** for a national industrial subsidy, using observable budget and local government capital/debt data? 2026 Sweden and Japan missions highlight unobserved cost incidence; OECD2025 calls for social cost-benefit/long-run metrics.

- Is NOT “world-first causal industrial subsidies paper”: extensive RDD/DID/GE evaluations exist (Criscuolo 2019, Cingano 2025, Bartelme 2025).
- Tight proposed result: municipality program COST incidence and contingent failure downside vs local benefit, not only treated-local employment. Requires **public source data audit and credible treatment/control**; merely making a dashboard is not causal contribution.
- **Status: OPEN POLICY EVIDENCE QUESTION, not identified theorem**.

### F-NO (NO-GO trivial baseline)
- 'Low government capacity -> do nothing': Hodler 2009 and IMF2024.
- 'Market integration cutoff local vs central industrial policy': Lin/Li 2026 quantitative paper.
- 'Cross-region fiscal externalities with local private information and national budget': Dai et al 2026.
- 'Co-financing share determines local participation': basic matching grant & 2018 transport infrastructure theory.
- 'Industry with big distortions targeted by national subsidy': Liu2019, Bartelme2025, WTO2026.
- 'National subsidies have positive local impact': local effect ≠ aggregate additionality; known.
