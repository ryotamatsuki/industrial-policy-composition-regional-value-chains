## 1. A genuine starting puzzle explicitly acknowledged by the strongest parent
Ernest Liu (2019), "Industrial Policies in Production Networks", Quarterly Journal of Economics 134(4), 1883–1948, DOI 10.1093/qje/qjz024; full primary PDF accessible:
https://ernestliu.scholar.princeton.edu/sites/g/files/toruqf4426/files/ernestliu/files/qjz024.pdf

**Exactly verified original contribution:** Proposition 1 (printed p.1899; PDF 0-based p.16): from zero subsidies and for the author's decentralized (i.e. nonintervention) production equilibrium, the social marginal value of targeted sector input subsidy spending is the sector-specific distortion centrality \`xi_i\`. Proposition 2 (p.1900) links first-order aggregate output response to covariance between distortion centrality and sector policy expenditure. The paper also offers Proposition 3/special Cobb-Douglas and nonmarginal intervention results: DO NOT falsely claim all Liu results are only first-order. The sufficiency statistic depends on a nonintervention baseline (endogenous policy-induced network budget changes are second order there).
**Explicit gap in the PRIMARY ARTICLE:** introduction printed p.1887 and conclusion p.1943: the author states that the analysis does *not address governments' decision process behind sectoral policy adoption*, and abstracts from political-economy factors. This is an author's declared scope limitation, **NOT a claim that subsequent global literature leaves the question unanswered**.

Economically sharper puzzle: a sector may create a large national marginal network benefit, but different local governments pay for policy subsidies with their own finite fiscal budgets and capture potentially different parts of network income. Private supplier choice can rewire the network after governments choose support. **Does such endogenous policy–network feedback invalidate local adoption of nationally optimal targeting, and if so, exactly when?**

Critically, the question must ALSO accommodate the possibility of **efficient decentralization** rather than assume an unavoidable externality. Ogawa & Wildasin (2009), AER 99(4), 1206–1217, DOI 10.1257/aer.99.4.1206, show some heterogeneous spillover economies yield efficient decentralized policies without upper-tier corrections. A new claim of "local spillovers imply decentralization fails" is dead on arrival.

## 3. Observed phenomenon, candidate explanation and actors kept separate
**Theoretical puzzle:** national and regional valuations of the same firm-sector policy can differ even with market-clearing firm transactions; whether that difference is eliminated by prices/spillbacks or amplified by private supplier relinking is unknown in the proposed complete strategic model. NOT an empirical claim that real regions imitate or misallocate subsidies.
**Candidate explanation:** local governments tax local residents and choose targeted sector subsidy portfolios before firms choose supplier relationships; firms internalize private revenue and input costs, while an inter-regional network intervention may generate geographic spillbacks and indirect, possibly nonsmooth changes in downstream prices/profits. Existing general subsidy or fiscal sharing corrections must not be assumed absent or costless.
**Essential actors:** two jurisdictions with local resident/taxpayer interests; competitive/monopolistically distorted firms with actual input supply choice; optionally one center as a **benchmark**, not a gratuitous strategic actor.
**Essential choices:** government policy-vector allocation across sectors under explicit local real budget constraints; firm link choice across regions after policies; factor prices, income incidence and government fiscal accounts determined from microfoundations.
**Outcomes:** equilibrium active supplier graph, policy target ranking, local and national real welfare, existence/uniqueness/multiplicity, explicit efficient vs inefficient decentralization regimes.
**Not required at Stage 0:** an instrument to repair a distortion, digital dataset, international trade gravity estimates, disaster scenarios, informational asymmetry, complicated transfer contracts, or a numerical estimate of effect.

## 4. One-sentence falsifiable main research question
"Under what conditions does endogenous *supplier-link switching* make the sector-ranking of independently optimizing, locally budget-constrained industrial-policy authorities depart from (or coincide with) the national welfare ranking identified in a production-network economy, in a way not implied by fixed-network fiscal competition or a single national policymaker?"

No claim that such a parameter region exists. A Stage-1/2 finding that the question is an immediate instance of Ogawa–Wildasin/Armbruster–Hintermann, Poirier, or a standard network game -> STOP.
