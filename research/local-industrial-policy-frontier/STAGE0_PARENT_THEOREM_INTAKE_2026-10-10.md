## 1. Parent-paper dossier A — Regional administrative state capacity
**Anchor A:** Bellofatto & Besfamille (2018), *Regional state capacity and the optimal degree of fiscal decentralization*, Journal of Public Economics 159:225–243, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpubeco.2017.12.010. **Full precursor PDF** Nov 3 2014 original https://eml.berkeley.edu/~webfac/auerbach/Finalversion.pdf read at MODEL §2, pp.4–6; planner §3 p.7; Prop.1 p.8, Prop.3 p.11, Prop.5 p.13, Prop.6 pp.16–17; proof §9.1 and source PDF figure screenshot. Not misrepresented as exactly identical to published 2018 version.

Parent primitives: L>=2 ex ante symmetric benevolent regions, initial project cost c, two possible completion stages; administrative capacity is **EXOGENOUS completion probability π∈[0,1]**, with local early benefit B and delayed benefit b<B, late refinancing cost c; fiscal capacity **EXOGENOUS share θ∈[0,1]** of available local tax base, with imperfectly mobile capital and a national bailout option. If partially decentralized, projects are refinanced by uniform national taxes shared across residents; with full decentralization, regions refinance by own mobile-capital tax. Efficient investment cutoff (2014 WP p.7):
c*(π) = [π B+(1−π)b]/(2−π).
**Proposition 1**: partial-decentralized project initiation iff
c <= c_PD(π) = L[π B+(1−π)b]/(L+1−π).
This cutoff is ABOVE the efficient c*, producing standard overinvestment from common pool. Proposition 3: if all regions refinance, symmetric pure Nash tax rate τ=c/(θ κ), where κ is regional resident capital. Proposition 6: choice of institutional regime depends on administrative and fiscal capacities; low admin capacity can favor full decentralization if sufficient fiscal capacity, contrary to naive "more admin capacity always favors decentralization."

**Already resolved:** administration affects success rates, central bailout moral hazard, local tax competition, optimal delegation regimes, region project thresholds. The generic idea "government administrative capacity matters for industrial policy" is NOT novel. **Specified scope gap:** neither the fully worked 2014 precursor nor published abstract models the LOCAL government's ability to SELECT how many innovation firms to investigate or to choose scarce case-worker verification effort, which might change precision, firm entry and who gets supported. Exogenous π cannot represent endogenous within-program screening allocation. **Serious absorption danger:** general principal–agent/multitasking/screening and congestion models already solve many versions; merely substituting π(n) is a trivial comparative static. 
**Verdict:** NO-GO as distinct Stage0 headline; merge *capacity scarcity* into the more specific innovation-procurement screening problem below, only if it generates a new contract tradeoff.

## 2. Parent-paper dossier B — Dynamic regional diversification and capabilities
**Anchor B1:** Alshamsi, Pinheiro & Hidalgo (2018), *Optimal diversification strategies in the networks of related products and of related research areas*, Nature Communications 9:1328, ORIGINAL OPEN ARTICLE https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-018-03740-9 and https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5889401/. Eq (1) specifies probability that an activity i becomes active:
p_i = B * ((sum_j a_ij M_j)/k_i)^α,
where a_ij is activity relatedness, M_j the already-developed activity indicator, k_i the count of related activities, and α the dependence intensity. Their controller chooses the sequence of activity targets, minimizing time to activate the network. They analyze network hubs, greedy vs majority rules and show the optimal time window for high-degree choices. **Crucially** this is ALREADY an optimization of how to sequence related and unrelated local economic capabilities; F0's simple "future option value from supporting an unrelated sector" was overbroad and must be retracted as an open problem.
**Anchor B2:** Hidalgo & Stojkoski, Dec 11 2025, *Capability Accumulation and Conditional Convergence: Towards a Dynamic Theory of Economic Complexity*, arXiv 2512.10672, ORIGINAL 24-page full PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.10672, abstract/introduction/underlying model inspected with PDF screenshot. Establishes analytical boundary for conditional convergence in dynamic endogenous capability accumulation. It does **not** by itself supply an institutionally constrained regional government industrial-policy Nash, but is a new source threat to a "model endogenous capabilities" originality claim.
**Anchor B3:** Boschma (2025), *Designing Smart Specialization Policy: Relatedness, Unrelatedness, or What?*, handbook DOI 10.1093/oxfordhb/9780198866190.013.0010, conceptual regional relevance of industrial lock-in vs unrelated activities. **Pinheiro** (2025), *Relatedness and economic complexity as tools for industrial policy: Insights and limitations*, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.strueco.2024.09.019, critiques treating relatedness frequencies as causal/normative. Nomaler & Verspagen (2024), https://doi.org/10.1016/j.strueco.2024.05.019, empirical rather than complete welfare model, finds unrelated variety plays substantial role in diversification metrics.

**What is not solved in Alshamsi:** region government choice under general-equilibrium real financial cost, ownership/taxes and private irreversible capability investment; paper acknowledges no *strategic interactions between multiple diffusion processes*; this is not evidence such interactions are novel anywhere else. **But** simply adding a regional budget or another council to an existing diversification shortest-path problem is likely either constrained dynamic programming or well-known graph optimization. Any eventual theoretical result must differ from the pre-existing optimal *target ordering*, not merely the order's sensitivity to arbitrary subsidy costs. 
**Verdict:** generic S01 "related vs unrelated optimal sequence" **NO-GO**; different narrowly defined question MAY re-enter separate Stage0 only after full dynamic GE parent audit, not within current programme.

## 3. Parent-paper dossier C — Local innovation procurement, government buyer capacity and contracting
**Anchor C1:** Hwang (2024), *A Theoretical Analysis of Public Procurement for Innovation*, KDI Journal of Economic Policy 46(2):21–43, https://doi.org/10.23895/kdijep.2024.46.2.21. ORIGINAL FULL OPEN HTML https://kdijep.org/v.46/2/21/A+Theoretical+Analysis+of+Public+Procurement+for+Innovation?view=print, §§I–II and Prop.1/Prop.2 inspected (some equation images require visual reinspection at Stage 1). Three players: ONE benevolent principal government, ONE procurement agent (SOE; author notes agent can be a local agency), private suppliers innovating products; fixed total quantity 1 split x innovative and 1−x standardized. Innovative quality y=m'+ε where variance σ², baseline quality m. Improvement Δ=m'−m>0; additional innovation cost δ=c'−c>0. Government commits to a payment/contract menu (α,β,b,p) and agent optimizes procurement x. Prop 1 solves x* under given contract, Prop 2 solves government-optimal incentive payment, participation constraints, and social welfare, explicitly handling optimal risk-sharing/volume-versus-quality compensation. **Already solved:** procurement-risk tradeoff, quality vs cost screening and principal-agent reward. Generic "government should buy prototypes" or "PPI risk can be shared" KILLED. Hwang's SOE has no separate ENDOGENOUSLY ACQUIRED verification technology whose limited staff time affects ex ante private R&D efforts and opportunity cost of other procurements; but this alone does not establish a new claim because dynamic moral hazard theory may cover it.

**Anchor C2:** Chiappinelli, Giuffrida & Spagnolo (2025), *Public procurement as an innovation policy: Where do we stand?*, International Journal of Industrial Organization 100:103157, ORIGINAL OPEN 20-page complete review https://diposit.ub.edu/bitstreams/25ce7f51-439b-4020-9031-97b7747f25e3/download (pp.10–14, especially Table 1 PDF p.13, image inspected). **Exact author-explicit future-theory gap**: Section 5 and Table 1 say *further theoretical work is needed to investigate buyer–innovator relationships*, especially public buyer officials' limited capacity, conflicting incentives, risk-management capability, and asymmetric information; the review's existing evidence on this implementation barrier is described as predominantly EMPIRICAL. It cites Giuffrida & Raiteri (2021) on officer workload and Decarolis et al. (2021) on manager effects, both retrospective external evidence not used as empirical work in current project. The review also notes joint procurement's aggregate-demand effect (p.9), so **demand pooling alone is known** and is not the main selected mechanism.
**Anchor C3:** economic threats: Rogerson (1989, 1994) contracts for R&D procurement; Iossa et al. (2018); Fabra and Montero (2022) optimal procurement with several technologies; Iossa/De Chiara (2019) public procurement innovation; classical joint moral hazard + delegation + staff screening/attention models; additional 2023 asymmetric-information innovative procurement contracts. Need full source equations at Stage1 before any novel claim.

**New Stage0 economic question (the only candidate SELECTED FOR PARENT-THEOREM AUDIT):**
"WHEN a local-government buyer must allocate scarce verification/monitoring effort BETWEEN developing an unproven local innovative supplier and retaining a proven conventional option, can the socially optimal contract combine quality information production and private innovative investment without inducing the buyer to sacrifice information quality for immediate procurement performance? When does optimal innovation procurement differ qualitatively from Hwang's single-principal fixed-quality-distribution risk-sharing outcome?"

This is a precise INTERACTION HYPOTHESIS, not a solved model: buyer's endogenous information technology and investment decision *jointly determine* both firm continuation and verifiable outcomes. Core issue is not the number of firms or a generic monitoring budget. A true distinctive theorem would require a structural reversal or institutional nonimplementability under Hwang's menu that standard one-agent delegated screening cannot immediately replicate. "Monitor more when precision valuable" alone FAILS. 
**Local policy relevance:** the local procurer actually chooses firm/prototype continuation using staff and budget and must face local resident welfare; if the outcome proves identical to generic national procurement under relabeling, NONLOCAL novelty kills this dissertation/paper question.
**Why one city, not joint municipal consortium now:** additional municipalities, consortium formation and heterogeneous demand create extra game forms but are not required for the core previously-stated buyer-innovator information failure, and could obscure the novelty gate. Pooling is retained as a future distinct parent issue, not stacked onto the base.
**Verdict:** CONDITIONAL GO FOR STAGE1 PRIMARY SOURCE COMPARISON ONLY. Prior art may fully absorb; no theorem or model constructed.
