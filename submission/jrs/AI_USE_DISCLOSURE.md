# AI-use disclosure for JRS submission

Stage 13 policy check date: 2026-09-07.

## Current Wiley requirement

Wiley's publishing-ethics guidance states that material use of AI technology in developing a manuscript must be disclosed transparently and in detail in the Methods section, a disclosure, or the Acknowledgments as appropriate. AI tools cannot be authors. Human authors remain accountable for the submission and should document the purpose of AI use and how outputs were reviewed and verified. Pure spelling, grammar, and general editing are outside the disclosure requirement, but this project used AI more substantively and therefore should disclose it.

Official policy source checked at Stage 13:
- Wiley, *Best Practice Guidelines on Publishing Ethics*, Artificial Intelligence section: https://authorservices.wiley.com/ethics-guidelines/

## Proposed manuscript disclosure

> **Use of artificial intelligence.** OpenAI ChatGPT was used as an AI-assisted tool for literature discovery, algebraic and code cross-checking, and manuscript drafting and editing. AI-generated outputs were not treated as evidence: cited sources were checked against source materials, and mathematical claims were checked against the frozen theory record, symbolic verification, and regression tests. The author retains full responsibility for the final content.

## Submission-system disclosure

If the Wiley submission system asks a separate AI-use question, answer consistently with the manuscript statement. Do not characterize the use as only spelling/grammar assistance.

## Author confirmation required before Stage 14 completion

The corresponding author should confirm that the proposed disclosure accurately describes the actual use and that the final manuscript, citations, proofs, and code have been reviewed to the level the author is prepared to take responsibility for. If additional AI tools materially contributed, add them before submission.
