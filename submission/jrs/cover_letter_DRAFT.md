The paper studies a regional-policy problem in which local governments allocate a fixed policy capacity across activities that are complementary across jurisdictions. The baseline model provides an exact characterization of decentralized portfolios and a coordinated fixed-capacity benchmark. Its central result is a positive-measure parameter interval in which both governments uniquely retain the same locally attractive priority even though coordinated reallocation of the same total policy capacity raises modeled aggregate surplus.

The paper is designed as a transparent analytical contribution to regional economics. It does not claim that interjurisdictional spillovers, fixed budgets, or public-policy composition are individually new. Rather, the contribution is the strategic combination of a binding local policy portfolio, vertically complementary regional activities, and incomplete jurisdictional capture, which generates an exact duplication-versus-reallocation threshold result. The manuscript also identifies the scope of that result through a restricted constant-returns extension and an alternative matching technology.
